Bringing Karasu Lake back to life

Since 2017 EFRA has been restoring Karasu Lake and the Praviy Yesentai river in north-west Almaty — together with residents, scientists, business and the city.

Project history Volunteer
Karasu Lake, Almaty

Since 2017 we have worked with the 9 700 residents of Karasu. The project brought ≈ $2 million of city investment to the park and the lake clean-up — the lake where a 21 km river begins.

Caring for nature starts with the lake next door

Karasu is EFRA's first and longest-running project. We started here in 2017, and here we test the approaches we want to bring to other water bodies in Almaty.

We believe that nature is the source of life and that people are responsible for protecting it. Anyone can contribute by taking care of a lake, a tree, a meadow or a small stream nearby. Our job is to educate, involve people in protecting nature and change how they relate to the environment around them.

Think globally, act locally

The lake where a river begins

Karasu Lake lies at the heart of the Karasu neighbourhood in north-west Almaty. Groundwater comes to the surface here, and this is where the Praviy Yesentai river begins.

The Praviy Yesentai is a small tributary of the Malaya Almatinka, which flows into the Kaskelen. These rivers water the farms of the Almaty region and, together with the Ili, feed the Kapshagay reservoir.

The story of the lake and our work

From the loss of the streams that fed the lake to a shared plan to keep it alive for decades to come.

  1. 2000–2010

    The lake loses its streams

    Uncontrolled construction and climate change dried up the two streams that fed the lake. Since then it has lived on groundwater alone.

  2. 2017–2019grants from Soros Foundation Kazakhstan (~$23,000) and the British Council (€5,000)

    Community and a park design

    We brought residents together, improved part of the area and prepared a full set of documents for its regeneration. Architect Asel Yeszhanova designed the park by the lake.

  3. 2021–2022

    The city builds the park

    Following our design, the Almaty akimat built a green zone and an amphitheatre by the lake. Together with the later clean-up, this is about $2 million of city investment.

  4. 2023–2024

    Clean-up and the loss of an ecosystem

    In November 2023 the city drained the lake for mechanical cleaning. Some of its wildlife moved to nearby water — the pond by the church and the gabion channel of the Praviy Yesentai — and the rest died.

  5. 2025

    Restoration

    With the pumping station we are raising the water level, adding chlorella, restocking fish and aerating the lake in winter. We are preparing a conservation plan and a climate adaptation plan for the lake.

  6. 2025–2030

    Long-term strategy

    We will carry out the conservation and adaptation plans, set up a committee for the sustainable development of the lake ecosystem and start restoring the Praviy Yesentai riverbed.

Who lived in the lake before 2021

We want to bring back everyone who lived here — first of all two species listed as Vulnerable on the IUCN Red List.

Vulnerable species

The broad-fingered crayfish and the Central Asian tortoise are the main restoration targets after 2025.

A dangerous predator

The snakehead wipes out other species, so it is being removed from the lake ecosystem.

More birds

Clean water and living shores will attract more seasonal birds.

The lake is the heart of the neighbourhood

Around the lake are a church, a mosque, a square and a park. It is a place to rest and meet for 9,700 residents — Russians, Koreans, Turks, Uyghurs, Germans and many others.

We want to keep the lake as part of this architectural ensemble and a place where neighbours spend time together.

Three lines of work

Restoring the lake

  • Raising the water level
  • Bringing back plants and wildlife
  • Feeding, monitoring, winter aeration

Plans for the future

  • Climate adaptation plan
  • Conservation plan for the lake as a natural site
  • Quarterly water analysis

People and education

  • Involving residents in caring for the lake
  • Environmental education for young people, including Ramsar Heroes
  • A sustainable development committee for the lake

Karasu Lake Conservation Plan. Between 2002 and 2024 the green space around the lake shrank from 13 to 7 hectares. The plan proposes boundaries for the remaining nature, a semi-natural park with wildlife refuges, a living shoreline and native plants.
